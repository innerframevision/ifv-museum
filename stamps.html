INNER FRAME VISION

INNER FRAME CONSTRUCTS

THE PHILATELIC TRANSFORMATION

The exhibition explores the postage stamp as a transformable visual structure.

A stamp traditionally contains an image within a small, strictly defined format. Its perforated edge marks a clear boundary between the image and the outside world. Within Inner Frame Constructs, this familiar object becomes the starting point for an entirely different architecture.

The stamp is treated as a visual puzzle.

Its internal composition can separate into fragments, shift, expand, and reorganize. Elements that initially exist as parts of a miniature image gradually become independent pictorial spaces.

Through this transformation, the scale of the work changes. What begins as a compact philatelic object unfolds into a sequence of paintings.

The perforation remains the memory of the original object, while the image moves beyond its initial proportions and boundaries.

Each transformation follows the architecture already hidden inside the stamp. A figure, landscape, building, object, or fragment can become the center of a new composition, while neighbouring elements develop into separate works.

The stamp therefore functions simultaneously as an object, a frame, a puzzle, and a compressed visual system.

The artwork does not simply appear inside the frame.

The frame itself unfolds into the artwork.
